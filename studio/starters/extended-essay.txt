# Introduction

Introduce your topic and state your research question. Explain why it is worth investigating and what a reader needs to know before the argument begins.

Outline how the essay answers the question: the sources, data or methods you used and the order of the sections that follow.

# Background

Summarise the theory, context and earlier research your essay builds on. Define key terms and cite the sources you rely on [@source2024].

# Methodology

Describe how you gathered your evidence: primary data, secondary sources, experiments or texts. Justify why this approach suits the research question and note its limits.

# Analysis and discussion

## First line of argument

Present your first piece of evidence and analyse what it shows. Link every point back to the research question.

## Second line of argument

Develop the argument with further evidence. Compare interpretations and explain where sources agree or disagree.

## Counter-arguments

Consider other views and the weaknesses in your evidence. Explain how they affect your answer.

# Evaluation

Evaluate the reliability of your sources and methods. Explain what you would change and which questions remain open.

# Conclusion

Answer the research question directly, drawing together the key findings. Do not introduce new evidence here.
